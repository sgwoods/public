Steven Gregory Woods

Neutral browser-readable draft based on the current research archive. This page is intended for review of tone, structure, and sourcing discipline before any possible Wikipedia editing.

Steven Gregory Woods is a Canadian technology executive, entrepreneur, investor, and former engineering leader at Google Canada. He is investing partner and chief technology officer at Inovia Capital and previously co-founded Quack.com, Kinitos, and NeoEdge Networks.[1][2][3]

Woods studied computer science at the University of Saskatchewan and completed graduate work in mathematics at the University of Waterloo, where he earned M.Math and PhD degrees in work connected to computer science and artificial intelligence. He later held research roles including postdoctoral work at the University of Hawai'i at Manoa and a period at the Software Engineering Institute in Pittsburgh.[4][5][6]

Education and research

Woods earned a Bachelor of Science in computer science from the University of Saskatchewan. He later pursued graduate study at the University of Waterloo, completing M.Math and PhD degrees in mathematics with work tied closely to computer science and artificial intelligence.[4][5]

His academic and research work has included postdoctoral study at the University of Hawai'i at Manoa and software engineering research associated with the Software Engineering Institute. Publications from this period focused on program understanding, software architecture, and design recovery.[6][11]

Business career

Quack.com

During the late 1990s Woods co-founded Quack.com, an early voice systems company later associated with AOL's acquisition of the business in 2000. Contemporary press coverage treated the company as part of the emerging voice portal market.[7][8]

Kinitos and NeoEdge

After Quack, Woods co-founded Kinitos. The company later evolved into NeoEdge Networks as its product and market focus shifted over time. Reporting from that period connects Woods to software services, gaming, and advertising technology activities associated with NeoEdge.[9]

Google Canada

From 2008 to 2021 Woods was part of Google Canada's engineering leadership. Public institutional and media sources describe him as a senior engineering leader during a period of major growth in Google's Canadian technical organization.[2][5][10]

Inovia Capital

In 2021 Woods joined Inovia Capital as partner and CTO. In that role he has been presented as working across founder support, technology evaluation, and company-building within the firm's investment platform.[1][3]

Publications, patents, and recognition

Woods has published research in software engineering and program understanding, including the 1997 book Constraint-Based Design Recovery for Software Reengineering: Theory and Experiments. He has also been listed on patents related to voice systems, web analysis, and structured data.[11][12]

His recognitions include the IEEE Working Conference on Reverse Engineering Outstanding Contribution Award, the J.W. Graham Medal from the University of Waterloo, and the University of Saskatchewan Lifetime Achievement Award.[4][5]
