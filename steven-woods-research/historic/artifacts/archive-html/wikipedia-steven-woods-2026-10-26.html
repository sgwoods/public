Steven Gregory Woods (born June 16, 1965) is a Canadian (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians)  entrepreneur. He is best known for co-founding Quack.com (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quack.com) , the first popular Voice portal (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_portal)  platform, in 1998. Woods became the head of engineering for Google (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google)  Canada where he was until 2021, when he joined Canadian Venture capital (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital)  firm iNovia Capital as partner and CTO,[1] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-:0-1)  following in the footsteps of Patrick Pichette (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Pichette) , Google's CFO who also joined iNovia after leaving Google.

Career

[edit (https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Steven_Woods&action=edit&section=1) ]

Born in Melfort, Saskatchewan (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melfort,_Saskatchewan) , Woods holds a Ph.D. and M.Math. from the David R. Cheriton School of Computer Science (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_R._Cheriton_School_of_Computer_Science)  at the University of Waterloo (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) [2] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-2)  in Canada (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada)  and a B.Sc. from the University of Saskatchewan (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Saskatchewan) . He was the first Ph.D. student of Professor Qiang Yang (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiang_Yang) . Woods' Ph.D. was published in 1997 as a book co-written with Alex Quilici (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Quilici)  and Qiang Yang (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiang_Yang)  entitled "Constraint-Based Design Recovery for Software Reengineering: Theory and Experiments"[3] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-3)  He then worked for Carnegie Mellon (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon) 's Software Engineering Institute (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_Engineering_Institute) [4] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-4)  on product line development and practical software architectural reconstruction and analysis.

In 1998 Woods co-founded Pittsburgh-based voice-portal infrastructure company Quackware (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quackware)  with Jeromy Carriere (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeromy_Carriere)  and Alex Quilici (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Quilici) . Quackware became Quack.com in 1999 and moved headquarters to Silicon Valley (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley) .[5] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-5)  It was funded September 1, 1999, and acquired August 31, 2000 by America Online (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_Online) .[6] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-6)  Quack.com claimed to be the first Voice Portal (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_Portal) , and held patents around interactive voice applications, including "System and method for voice access to internet-based information".[7] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-7)

Woods was vice president of voice services for America Online and Netscape (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netscape)  after the acquisition of Quack.com in September, 2000.[8] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-8)

He founded NeoEdge in 2002 under the name Kinitos (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinitos) [9] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-9)  along with former America Online (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_Online) , Netscape (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netscape) , and Quack.com (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quack.com)  colleagues including Jeromy Carriere (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeromy_Carriere) . He served as an officer and on the board of directors from 2001 through 2010 in Palo Alto (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto) , California (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California) .[10] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-10)

Woods was the engineering director for Google (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google)  in Canada from 2008 to 2021, where he joined Canadian Venture capital (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital)  firm iNovia Capital as partner and CTO.[1] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-:0-1) [11] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-11)

Achievements

[edit (https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Steven_Woods&action=edit&section=2) ]

While at the University of Waterloo, Woods captained the varsity Waterloo Warriors (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterloo_Warriors)  golf team and in 1995 was co-winner of the Ontario University Athletics (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_University_Athletics)  "Len Shore Trophy" for top individual performance.[12] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-12)  During 1991-1992 Woods was a member of the Canberra Knights (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra_Knights)  semi-professional ice hockey in the Australian Ice Hockey League (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Ice_Hockey_League) .[citation needed (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed) ] Woods was awarded the J.W. Graham Medal (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.W._Graham_Medal)  for Computing and Innovation by the University of Waterloo (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo)  in 2010.[13] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-13) [14] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Woods#cite_note-JWGrahamMedal-14)
