# Source Hierarchy

Use this hierarchy when researching a living person.

## Tier 1: Self-approved and official baseline sources

Use these first to establish identity, current role, rough timeline, and public link network:

- LinkedIn
- current employer profile
- official personal site
- bio.link / about.me
- university or institutional profile

These sources are often strong for:

- display name
- current role
- prior employers
- education
- geography
- outbound links

Do not rely on them alone for notability or controversial claims.

## Tier 2: Canonical subject-matter sources

Use these to confirm and enrich the record:

- publisher pages
- DOI pages
- patents.google.com
- dblp
- university repositories
- official conference pages
- official talk/video pages
- award pages

These are strong for:

- publications
- patents
- talks
- dates
- awards

## Tier 3: Independent reliable secondary sources

Use these for biography, significance, and notability:

- major press
- industry press
- reputable magazines
- newspapers
- substantial interviews
- third-party profiles with editorial standards

These are the most important sources for a Wikipedia-style article.

## Tier 4: Discovery-only or weak sources

Use these carefully and usually only as leads:

- SignalHire
- Wiza
- Clay
- profile mirrors
- scraped directories
- reposted wiki mirrors

These may help identify names, roles, or date ranges, but they should usually remain:

- internal research scaffolding
- gap-finding aids
- prompts for stronger follow-up sourcing

Do not treat them as durable final evidence unless there is a special reason and you note the limitation explicitly.

## Claim categories

For each important claim, ask:

1. Is it self-presented?
2. Is it official and canonical?
3. Is it independently reported?
4. Is it only weakly mirrored?

Prefer to label claims internally as:

- `baseline`
- `corroborated`
- `independent`
- `weak`
- `rejected`

## Timeline rules

A person archive should keep a timeline even before everything is perfect.

Good timeline anchors:

- degree years
- employment start/end years
- company founding dates
- acquisition or funding dates
- award years
- public appearance dates

When dates conflict:

- prefer official or canonical dates
- record the conflict in notes
- avoid confident prose until resolved
