# Wikipedia Biography Drafts

Use this reference when preparing a Wikipedia-style biography draft for a living person.

Primary rules to respect:

- Biographies of living persons: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Biographies_of_living_persons
- Notability (people): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Notability_(people)
- Reliable sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Reliable_sources
- Manual of Style / Biography: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Biography
- Conflict of interest: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Conflict_of_interest
- Autobiography: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Autobiography

## Core constraints

For a living person:

- be conservative
- avoid promotional tone
- avoid synthesis that outruns the sources
- avoid resume-style dumping
- avoid using self-published material to establish notability

Wikipedia-style drafts should be built mainly from:

- multiple independent reliable secondary sources
- substantial coverage, not just routine mentions

## What counts as a good draft candidate

A strong candidate usually has:

- several independent sources that discuss the person in meaningful depth
- a clear reason the person matters beyond their own employer bio
- stable dates, roles, and institutional history
- enough sourcing to support a neutral lead and a short chronology

If these are missing, do not force a full article draft. Produce:

- a notability assessment
- a sourcing gap list
- a recommended outline only

## Draft structure

Keep drafts simple and neutral:

1. Lead
   - who the person is
   - why they are notable
   - current or most recognized role
2. Early life and education
   - only if well sourced
3. Career
   - use subsections by major phase if needed
4. Publications, patents, or recognitions
   - only if independently relevant and well sourced
5. References
   - map each important statement to a source family

## Style rules

- write in the third person
- avoid adjectives like `leading`, `renowned`, `visionary`, `prominent` unless directly and neutrally supported
- prefer plain claims with dates and institutions
- avoid long bullet lists in the article body
- include only material that matters to the subject's significance

## COI and autobiography caution

If the draft is being prepared by or for someone close to the subject:

- flag that COI/autobiography issues may apply
- recommend review by uninvolved editors before publication
- prefer talk-page style suggestions over direct publication claims

## Output pattern

When asked for a Wikipedia-style draft, produce:

1. `Assessment`
   - article-ready
   - draftable but needs stronger sourcing
   - not article-ready
2. `Support summary`
   - strongest independent sources
   - strongest primary sources
   - weak or excluded sources
3. `Draft`
   - only if the support is strong enough
4. `Claims to exclude`
   - items that should stay out until better sourced
